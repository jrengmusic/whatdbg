## Overview

This part gives whatdbg the three things that Parts 4 to 6 build on. The project gets its lexicon, `namespace Id`, with each row that the later parts use. `dap::Types` becomes one object with one `.cpp`. The request queue of `dap::Reader` becomes `dap::FIFO`, the one lane type of the rewrite.
The hand-written loop and `debug::State` stay in this part. The behavior stays, except difference D3 for the request lane.

## What this part does not do

- No message loop, no `jam::Model` object, and no second context. Part 6.
- No change of a handler body, a drain, or an engine call, except the names of the words.

### Step 4: `dap::FIFO`
**Scope:** new `Source/dap/FIFO.h` and `Source/dap/FIFO.cpp`; `Source/dap/Reader.h` and `.cpp`; `Source/Whatdbg.cpp`.
**Action:**
1. Delete from `dap::Reader` first: the members `fifo` and `storage`, `tryPop`, and `addMessage` (`Reader.h:81-84`; `Reader.cpp:22`, `:46-55`, `:120-138`).
2. Write `class FIFO` in `namespace dap`:
   - `explicit FIFO (int capacity);` sizes `storage` to `capacity` and `fifo` to the same total size, as `Reader` does today (`Reader.cpp:22`; `Reader.h:81`).
   - `int getFreeSpace() const noexcept;` returns `fifo.getFreeSpace()`.
   - `void write (juce::var message);` asserts `getFreeSpace() > 0`. Inside the positive test it moves `message` into the slot that `fifo.prepareToWrite (1, ...)` gives and calls `fifo.finishedWrite (1)`. This is the form of `EVETerminalProcessor.cpp:132-138`. One writer thread at a time.
   - `template <typename Callback> void applyPendingData (Callback&& callback)` is in the header. It reads `fifo.getNumReady()` messages with `fifo.prepareToRead`, calls `callback (const juce::var&)` for each message of the two blocks in order, assigns a void `juce::var` to each slot that it read, and calls `fifo.finishedRead` with the count. This is the form of `jam_TerminalFIFO.h:94-111`. One reader thread.
   - Private members: `juce::AbstractFifo fifo;` and `std::vector<juce::var> storage;`. Each slot access is `.at()`.
3. `dap::Reader`: hold `dap::FIFO requests { fifoCapacity };`. The reader thread calls `requests.write (std::move (message))` where it called `addMessage` (`Reader.cpp:156`). Add the public member `template <typename Callback> void applyPendingData (Callback&& callback)`, which forwards to `requests`.
4. `Whatdbg::run` (`Whatdbg.cpp:73-74`): replace the `tryPop` loop with `reader.applyPendingData ([this] (const juce::var& message) { onCommand (message); });`.

**Validation:** `dap::FIFO` holds no lock and no allocation after its constructor. A full lane is an assert, and the message is not written (difference D3). `tryPop` and `addMessage` have no caller: the build is the proof. `FIFO.h` and `FIFO.cpp` hold no include other than what `Reader.h` holds today. The smoke suite passes with equal sequences.

## BLESSED Alignment

- **B — Bound.** `dap::FIFO` owns its storage. It has one writer thread and one reader thread.
- **L — Lean.** One lane type. One lexicon step holds each row of the PLAN.
- **E — Explicit.** Each protocol word is one generated name. A full lane asserts.
- **S — SSOT.** `cast/identifiers.md` is the one place of a word. The lane logic is in one type. `dap::Types` has one translation unit.
- **S — Stateless.** `dap::FIFO` holds messages in transit only. A read slot is cleared.
- **E — Encapsulation.** `Reader` exposes the read of its lane and no other part of it.
- **D — Deterministic.** The sequences of Part 1 are equal after each step.
- LANGUAGE.md: no override applies beyond the C++ / JUCE Lean adaptation.
